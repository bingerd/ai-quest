# Decide the argument before you make anything

The most expensive mistake in a deck is not a bad font. It is discovering on Thursday that the
story was wrong, after twenty slides have been designed around it.

Your profession has used the same fix for decades: settle the argument in plain text first. It is
called the Pyramid Principle, after Barbara Minto, who taught it at McKinsey. Two parts of it do
almost all the work.

## One: open with S-C-Q-A

/>

That last one is what people find hardest. The instinct is to build up to the recommendation, the
way you would in an essay. In a business deck it goes first, because a reader who agrees after
three slides should be allowed to stop.

## Two: make every title carry the message

A slide called **"Benefits tracking"** tells you what the slide is about. A slide called
**"Benefits are tracking at 2.1× programme cost, ahead of the 1.5× business case"** tells you what
to think.

The second kind is an *action title*. When all of them are action titles, someone can read only
the titles and get the whole argument — which is exactly what a partner does on the way into the
room, and what a client sponsor does afterwards when they forward it on.
