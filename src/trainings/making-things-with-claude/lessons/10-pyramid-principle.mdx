# Decide the argument before you make anything

The most expensive mistake in a deck is not a bad font. It is discovering on Thursday that the
story was wrong, after twenty slides have been designed around it.

Consultants have used the same fix for decades: settle the argument in plain text first. It is
called the Pyramid Principle, after Barbara Minto, who taught it at McKinsey. Two parts of it do
almost all the work.

## One: open with S-C-Q-A

    { id: 'q', label: 'Question', icon: '❓', detail: 'The question the complication forces. "Do we keep investing in mid-market?" Saying it out loud means the room is answering your question, not four different ones.' },
    { id: 'a', label: 'Answer', icon: '✅', detail: 'Your recommendation, before the evidence rather than after it. Everything that follows exists to support this sentence.' },
  ]}
/>

That last one is what people find hardest. The instinct is to build up to the recommendation, the
way you would in an essay. In a business deck it goes first, because a reader who agrees after
three slides should be allowed to stop.

## Two: make every title carry the message

A slide called **"Pipeline coverage"** tells you what the slide is about. A slide called
**"Pipeline covers 2.1× of target, below the 3× we plan against"** tells you what to think.

The second kind is an *action title*. When all of them are action titles, someone can read only
the titles and get the whole argument — which is exactly what a busy executive does.
