# Claude Design, and what it costs you

Claude Design lets you make visual work — designs, prototypes, one-pagers, slides — by talking
about it. It is in **beta**, on Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise, and not on Free.

Whether it is switched on depends on your plan. On Pro, Max and Team it is on by default. **On
Enterprise it is off until an owner turns it on** in Organization settings › Artifacts. If you
cannot find it, that is the first thing to check, and the next lesson covers what to do instead.

## The part people get wrong

There is a widely repeated belief that Claude Design has its own budget, so you can burn through it
without touching the rest of your work. That used to be true. It is not any more:

> Claude Design previously had its own weekly allowance, separate from your other usage limits. All
> Claude Design activity now counts toward your plan's shared limits.

Everything draws from one pool — claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Design. So a long
afternoon of design iterations is an afternoon taken out of everything else you needed to do.

<ConceptCard title="What actually drives the cost" icon="🔥" tone="warn">
  Anthropic does not publish numbers, but it is explicit about the mechanism: *"complex projects
  with large codebases or many iterations consume more usage."* Two things you control, then —
  **how much** you are making, and **how many times** you ask for it to change. The next lesson
  lets you push both.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptReveal prompt="You hit your usage limit halfway through a design. What happens to the work?">
  The work is fine; you are the one who stops. Claude Design becomes unavailable until your limits
  reset. If your plan has usage credits enabled you can carry on past the included limits. The
  practical lesson is to check Settings › Usage *before* starting something big, not at four
  o'clock when it stops.
</ConceptReveal>
