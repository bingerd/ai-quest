# When you do not have Claude Design

Claude Design is in beta and, on Enterprise plans, off until an owner switches it on. That is not a
dead end. There are three ways to get a real deck out of Claude, and the right one depends on who
has to edit it afterwards.

| | Claude Design | A created .pptx | Claude for PowerPoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Status | Beta | Generally available | Generally available |
| Plans | Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise | Free and every paid plan | Pro, Max, Team, Enterprise |
| Where you work | A canvas in Claude | An ordinary chat | Inside PowerPoint itself |
| Follows your template | Via your design system | Not by itself | Reads the slide master directly |
| Best when | You are still exploring the look | You want a file and you want it now | The corporate template is non-negotiable |

## The rule that survives all three

Whichever surface you use, the corporate template goes on **first**. Anthropic's own guidance for
the PowerPoint add-in is to apply your template before generating content, be specific about which
slide or element you are changing, and review every change against your brand before it goes out.

<ConceptCard title="Match the format to whoever maintains it" icon="🔑">
  Interactive HTML is the best way to present a deck and the worst way to hand one over. If finance
  has to update it next quarter in PowerPoint, the deliverable is a .pptx, however good the HTML
  looks in the room.
</ConceptCard>

<ConceptReveal prompt="Someone forwards you a deck from outside the company and asks Claude to tidy it up. What is the risk?">
  Files from outside can carry instructions aimed at the assistant rather than at you. Anthropic is
  explicit about it for the PowerPoint add-in: use it with trusted files, because external files
  can contain hidden instructions that try to make it extract data or change records. Treat an
  unknown deck the way you would treat an unknown email attachment.
</ConceptReveal>

<ConceptCard title="Not for final deliverables on their own" icon="👀" tone="warn">
  Anthropic lists three things Claude for PowerPoint is not for: final client deliverables without
  human review, presentations with highly sensitive or regulated data without proper controls, and
  replacing your own judgement on design and narrative flow. The same applies to any of the three.
</ConceptCard>
