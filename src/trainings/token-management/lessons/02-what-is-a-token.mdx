# What is a token?

Models do not read characters or words. They read **tokens**: chunks of text that are usually a bit shorter than a word.

Rules of thumb that are good enough for planning:

| Text | Simulated tokens |
| --- | --- |
| One English word | about 1.3 |
| One page of text (~500 words) | about 700 |
| A 20-page report | about 14,000 |
| An hour-long meeting transcript | about 12,000 |

<ConceptReveal prompt="Which costs more: sending 10,000 tokens of input, or receiving 2,000 tokens of output?">
  Usually the **output**. Output tokens are typically priced 3 to 5 times higher than input tokens, and they take far longer to produce because the model generates them one at a time. Asking for shorter, more focused answers is one of the cheapest optimisations there is.
</ConceptReveal>

<ConceptCard title="Two meters are always running" icon="⏱️">
  Input tokens decide how much the model has to read. Output tokens decide how much it has to write. Cost and latency come from both. This simulator uses **tokens ≈ characters ÷ 4**. Real tokenizers vary, but the resource-management lesson is the same.
</ConceptCard>
